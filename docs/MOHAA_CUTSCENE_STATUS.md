# MOHAA in-game cutscenes

## m1l1 opening ride

**Tag data and path progression work in Dolphin. The split German actor loads and reaches draw submission in the matched m1l1 build; the saved capture does not verify its animation or cutscene motion. Both scripted trucks still fail to load in that build.**

The runtime log shows the retail m1l1 script loading 5 tagged models and 65 animation lengths, joining as Allies Rifleman, and reading a moving `truck2` tag path through to its endpoint. See [the tag-motion report and capture](../media/2026-11-22-m1l1-tag-arena/README.md).

The first render-binding pass is in the source and has compiled into a GameCube DOL from an isolated copy of the locally available HL1 engine source:

- `script_model` and `script_object` now spawn as `CBaseAnimating` entities; `script_origin` remains an invisible `info_target`.
- `c_model` still selects the baked tag data and now assigns an MDL to renderable script models when its TIKI appears in `maps/<map>.models`.
- The backend reads that manifest and precaches the MDLs in the GameCube `ServerActivate` hook before the first mission frame.
- The exporter keeps single-weight SKD skeletons and writes each TIKI animation alias as a Studio v10 sequence. Two body choices per surface let the runtime match TIKI init and per-alias `+nodraw`/`-nodraw` entry actions; the visible-surface bitmask is stored in the scripted model's sequence metadata. The `anim` callback selects the sequence and applies its bodygroups while preserving tag sampling and `animdone` timing.

The m1l1 German Afrika private is staged as a composite Studio model. The exporter merges the body, covered head, hands, helmet, loadout and KAR clip by bone name into a 72-bone rig and remaps its four-influence vertex weights. It keeps all ten aliases called on the actor by the retail script: `opel_driver`, the three `*_killvisa` aliases, the three `*_getvisa` aliases, and the three `*_idlevisa` aliases. The monolithic export is 552,324 bytes, with 3,400 triangles and nine bodygroups. The `scriptmodels` stage now splits it losslessly into a 177,632-byte MDL core, a 66,632-byte external skin, and a 308,864-byte GameCube animation pack. The pack retains all ten sequences, whose largest payload is 55,332 bytes. The staged `maps/m1l1.models` manifest includes this actor TIKI. On spawn, the MOHAA binding assigns a matching precached MDL to tracked mission actors; `model` and `anim` commands can update their Studio model, sequence and surface bodygroups. Scripted grunts remain under mission control while the TIKI model is active. Direct split output is under `/tmp/mohaa-german-actor-gca-experiment/`; runtime use of the split format still needs an engine build.

`tools/mohaa_tiki_model.py` bakes `vehicles/gmctruck_opening.tik` as a Studio v10 model with 52 bones, 3 sequences (`idle`, synthetic `idlenolights`, `open_gate`), 13 surface bodygroups and 5,171 triangles. The `scriptmodels` stage in `tools/mohaa_sd.py` writes both to the user's SD game directory and creates `maps/m1l1.models`. Retail-derived outputs stay on the SD card and are not committed.

The SKC reader decodes every per-bone frame, and both the tag baker and MDL exporter use that shared decoder. Retail data has `opel_opening.skc` at 6 frames / 10 fps and `opel.skc` at 1 frame / 25 fps. The converted TIKI visibility states now match the retail init and sequence entries: GMC `open_gate` reveals the scripted panels, Opel `idle`/`idlelights`/`idlenolights` switch the light surface, and wheel aliases switch between moving and still wheel meshes.

The vehicle meshes use single-bone weights. The exporter writes Xash's four-influence Studio v10 skin records, follows recursive TIKI animation includes, and can restrict an export to mission-used aliases. The German Afrika private composite replaces the earlier three-alias stage above with the complete ten-alias set used by `maps/m1l1.scr`; its model inspector output and `maps/m1l1.models` manifest are recorded above. ## Next implementation

The latest OSReport confirms 643 nodes, scripted `walkto` progression, and spill-loaded trucks. Damage-toggle execution still has no dedicated runtime indicator. Add a MOHAA-specific studio or map-arena allocation policy while preserving the DoD profile, then verify visible actor sequence playback and the opening cutscene. Once the shared engine checkout claim clears and its revision is pinned, carry the validated MOHAA model-admission, path-loading, and `models/vehicles/` spill changes into the canonical engine tree, then build a DOL and ISO from that pinned revision. Confirm stable visible trucks and actor, memory headroom, model precache, transform following, sequence playback, and bodygroup switching through the opening. The path-follow TIKIs are tag carriers with helper triangles; keep them as tag sources. Do not call the opening cutscene complete until a mid-sequence capture confirms the models and camera visually.

Physical GameCube playback remains a separate validation gate.
